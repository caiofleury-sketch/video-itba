## Color has a role

- Blue: remembered context, lags and averages; also the stable illustrative history.
- Amber: movement and change; also the illustrative recent-fall history. It is not a churn label.
- Teal: features relative to a customer's own baseline, including funds and usage.
- Rose: ratios or relative position, including utilization and monetary ranks.
- Violet: recent-weighted memory, including EMA.

In the results, explicit signs, labels, and marker types carry the outcome. Muted red marks negative AUC changes; amber marks mixed profit consistency. The identity glyph retains its original color so the viewer can recognize the feature even when its measured outcome differs from its hypothesis.

## Motion has a job

- Lag uses a ghosted current point and a backward marker: preserve the previous level.
- Delta uses a connecting segment and a change arrow: expose movement.
- Means use a settling reference line over several points: establish usual behavior.
- Relative funds and usage use paired reference/current bars: compare behavior with personal scale.
- Utilization uses a partially filled ring and division symbol: represent use relative to capacity.
- Ranks reorder points along an axis: compare position rather than raw magnitude.
- EMA uses progressively larger/brighter past points: emphasize recency without implying a hard cutoff.

The glyphs move from the feature map into the testing setup, then become chart-row identifiers. That preserves visual identity across scenes. Experimental values appear at their final coordinates; we do not animate a score counting upward as if it were a measured trajectory.
