We tested whether recent behavior helps predict leaving the Premium package. Each feature variant was retrained with the same learner settings and training rules, using five matched random seeds.

We compared last-month values, monthly changes, three-month averages, balance and payroll relative to their own recent averages, and card utilization. Only relative funds passed our predeclared selection rule.

After tuning, that ensemble scored about four hundred and two on Inicial, below our earlier reference. We then tested six-month history, monetary ranks, usage changes, and an exponential moving average.

We expected EMA to flag disengagement by emphasizing recent behavior. Six-month funds and three-month usage improved historical profit in both months. EMA’s profit benefit was inconsistent. These variants were never publicly scored.

Later engineered pipelines reached the Senior leaderboard. None beat our champion’s public score of about four hundred and fifty-four. That champion already includes extensive historical features.

Our recommendation: keep the rich-history champion. Prioritize controlled retests of six-month funds history and three-month usage. Keep EMA exploratory, and distinguish historical improvements from measured public performance.
